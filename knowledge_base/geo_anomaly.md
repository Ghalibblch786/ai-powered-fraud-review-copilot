# Geographic Anomaly

## What it is
A geographic anomaly is a transaction occurring somewhere meaningfully different from where the customer normally transacts — a different city or region than their registered home location or established pattern. Location alone is rarely definitive, but it's a strong corroborating signal when paired with anything else unusual.

## How it typically shows up
A compromised card is often used wherever the fraudster happens to be, which may have no relationship to the real cardholder's life. This shows up as a transaction in a location with no history for that customer, appearing abruptly with nothing in the account's recent activity (like a travel-related purchase) to explain it. It's especially notable when the new location is far enough from the customer's home base that ordinary travel wouldn't easily account for it, or when it appears alongside other odd activity rather than as an isolated one-off.

## Evidence signals
- Transaction location differs from the customer's registered home location
- No prior transaction history for that customer in this location
- No preceding travel-related activity (flights, hotels, etc.) that would explain the customer being there
- The location shift appearing alongside other unusual activity on the same account, rather than as a single isolated trip

## Innocent explanations
- People travel for holidays, work, or family, and some move house without updating their home location.
- A recent purchase in the travel category can explain being somewhere else.
- A purchase in another city with a normal amount, at a low-risk merchant, in a category the customer already uses is often just a trip.
- The system only knows city names, not the distance between them, so it cannot tell a nearby town from a far-away one. Do not claim a location is "far".

## What to check
- The transaction location compared with the customer's home location.
- Whether the customer has bought anything in this location before.
- Any recent travel-category purchases.
- The amount compared with the customer's typical spending range.
- The merchant's risk tier and category.
- Other unusual activity around the same time, such as several purchases close together or a new category.

## How to decide
- Lean **clear** when location is the only unusual thing: normal amount, low- or medium-risk merchant, usual category, established account.
- Lean **escalate** when the location is paired with another warning sign: an amount above the typical range, a high-risk merchant, a burst of purchases, a new category, or a very new account.
- Describe evidence as "consistent with" a pattern. Never state that fraud happened; the analyst makes the final decision.
